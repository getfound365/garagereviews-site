The verdict

This is the value pick of the day’s generator set: $799 buys 6,000 peak / 5,400 running watts on gasoline, a propane option that still makes 4,600 running watts, remote start on a key fob, and CO detection — a feature list that reads like a $999 unit. Buyers back it up: Rick (5 stars, Nov 7, 2026) runs a 50-amp motorhome off it and says it “starts right up on gas or propane,” and Dru (5 stars, Oct 28, 2026) calls it an “absolute bargain of the sale price for this feature filled powerhouse” after a five-hour break-in, and had an electrician add an interlock for home backup.

The honest catch is the review base: 36 ratings is thin next to the Champion 6500W’s 115, and 67 dB is louder than the quietest inverters in the class — the Champion runs 64 dB(A) and the WEN DF451i runs 58 dBA at quarter load. It also uses “a little more fuel” than a smaller Predator 3500, per Rick, which is the honest price of 5,400 running watts. If you want the deepest buyer record and the quietest run, the Champion 6500W at $999 is $200 more. If you want dual fuel, remote start, and $799, this is the pick.

Who it’s for

RVers and homeowners who want to start the generator without going outside in the weather — the remote start is the feature that earns its keep in a storm — and who want propane as a second fuel when gas stations lose power. The TT-30R outlet plugs straight into a camper, the 14-50R outlet covers a 50-amp RV or a transfer switch, and 5,400 running watts covers a fridge, furnace fan, lights, and a microwave at once. It is not for anyone chasing the quietest inverter in the class — 67 dB is middling — and it is not a whole-home unit: 5,400 running watts will not carry central air.

Performance in real use

The buyer evidence is thin but unanimous at the top: the newest reviews are both 5 stars, and both are from people actually using it. Rick (5 stars, Nov 7, 2026) runs a 50-amp motorhome and notes it “does use a little more fuel than my Predator 3500 but it puts out all the power I need.” Dru (5 stars, Oct 28, 2026) bought it as a first full-size generator and first inverter, ran a five-hour break-in, and had an electrician add an interlock — the exact home-backup setup this unit is built for. On paper, the 3-gallon tank gives 6.5 hours at 50% load on gasoline, and about 7.5 hours on a 20-pound propane tank per independent spec guides — enough for an evening, not a full night, so plan a refuel or a second tank for a multi-day outage. The inverter output is under 3% THD, safe for laptops and phones, per independent spec guides.

Setup

Three ways to start: wireless remote (key fob included), electric push-button, or recoil pull cord as backup. Add oil — it is included — and gas, or hook up a propane tank; the LCD display shows watts, voltage, frequency, runtime, and fuel level. The low-idle switch stretches fuel when the load is light, and the unit is parallel capable (kit sold separately) if you ever want to pair two. CO detection with auto shutoff is built in, but it is a backup, not a plan: run it outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house, exhaust away from windows and doors, never in a garage, and keep a battery CO alarm in the house whenever a generator is running.
