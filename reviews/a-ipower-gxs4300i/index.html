The verdict

On the evidence available, this is the best-rated inverter in the batch — 4.9 stars — with the important caveat that the pool is thin (70 ratings) and the noise rating is disputed: A-iPower’s own spec sheet states 62 dB at half load, while the Walmart listing advertises 52 dBA. Do not buy this one for quiet campground use on the 52 dBA claim alone. Best for: home backup and RV use where 3,450 running watts, a CO sensor, and electric start matter more than a quiet rating — and where the buyer accepts a thinner review pool than the Westinghouse alternatives.

Who it's for

Homeowners who want a mid-size inverter for fridge, freezer, lights, and chargers, and RVers who need 3,450 running watts with a CO sensor and electric start. Not for: anyone who needs a verified quiet rating (the noise figure is disputed), or whole-home backup — 3,450 running watts is a light-load machine.

Performance in real use

Buyers in the newest 25 reviews are near-unanimous: 21 five-star, 2 four-star, 2 three-star, and no low reviews. “I highly recommend this generator for home backup,” wrote Michele (Nov 5, 2026). “The unit arrived ahead of schedule,” wrote John (Oct 27, 2026), and another buyer reported simply that it “works well” (Patience, Oct 2, 2026). No buyer in the newest 25 reports a no-start, a noise complaint, or a power problem. The thin pool is the honest caveat: 70 ratings is not 6,300, and a 4.9 average on 70 ratings is less proven than a 4.7 on 6,300.

Setup

Per A-iPower: add SAE 10W-30 oil, add unleaded gasoline, and electric-start (or recoil-start as backup). The LED display shows runtime, fuel level, and output. The wheel kit and telescopic handle make it a roll-out unit rather than a carry unit at 48.9 lb. Break-in guidance mirrors the class: keep loads modest in the first hours.

Against the Westinghouse iGen2500 at $450, the GXS4300i gives you 1,250 more running watts and a CO sensor at $50 more — but the iGen2500 is out of stock at Walmart, and its 6,300-rating pool is far deeper than this unit’s 70.
