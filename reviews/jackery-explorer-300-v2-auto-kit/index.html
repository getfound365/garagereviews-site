Who it's for

Anyone who wants a small, light power station for a car, a campsite, or a short outage — phones, a laptop, a CPAP for part of a night — and who will buy while the 4 last. Skip it if you need to run a fridge or a full day of loads: 288Wh is hours, not a day. Skip it if you wanted solar in the box: this kit has no panels.

Performance in real use

The 288Wh LiFePO4 battery is rated for 4,000+ cycles — years of use — with 300 watts continuous and a 600-watt surge, two AC outlets, and 100W USB-C PD for a laptop. The UPS switchover is under 20 milliseconds, so a plugged-in router or CPAP keeps running through a blip. At 8.1 pounds it fits in a car trunk or a backpack. The 12V auto-charging cable charges it from a car battery — the point of the Auto Kit. Buyers like the deal and the delivery; the complaints are about the cables and the “solar generator” wording on the box, not the station itself.

Setup

Charge it to 100% before the first trip — from the wall, a car, or a solar panel (sold separately). The 12V cable connects to a car battery for charging on the road; read the cable instructions before the first use, because a car-battery connection is not a USB plug. Keep it between charges if it sits unused: top it up every few months so the battery stays healthy.
