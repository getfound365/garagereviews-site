Performance in real use

The tower is a system, not a box. The three pieces — toolbox (DS165), XL toolbox (DS400), and rolling toolbox (DS450) — stack and lock with auto-connect side latches, so you roll the whole kit in on the 8-inch wheels and split it apart at the job. The IP65 rating covers dust and high-pressure water jets, the 2-piece metal front latches are a step up from plastic, and the metal-reinforced padlock eye is the security point. At about 40 lb empty and 250 lb of load capacity, it carries a full kit without flexing — one reviewer reports stacking over 200 lb of tools on the rolling base without drama. The honest catch is in the newest reviews: the release clips are the most-reported failure (“the boxes clip together fine, but when you try to release them the clips won't stay open”), the handle does not drop all the way, the wheels protrude, and the lid does not stay open on its own. Those are annoyances, not breakages, but they are the reason this is 4.1 stars and not 4.5.

Setup

There is almost nothing to assemble: stack the three boxes, click the auto-connect latches, and roll. Load the heavy tools low in the rolling base and the daily-use tools in the top box, and test every latch before the first job — open and close each box, release the stack, and check the handle drops fully, because those are the exact failure points buyers report. The tower is backwards compatible with most ToughSystem 1.0 boxes and drawers (not the 1.0 radio), so existing DeWalt boxes click onto the same stack. And remember the listing warning: until the Walmart listing shows the three-piece tower again, do not order expecting three boxes — it currently shows the single XL box.
