The verdict

Buy it if you want the lightest real power station in the roster: 128Wh of LiFePO4, a 200W pure sine wave inverter, a 100W USB-C port, and a 10ms UPS, in a box that fits in a backpack and weighs less than a full water bottle. Imran (5 stars) calls it “the best most compact solar generator I have seen” and adds, “Charges all our phones quickly. This even has a light bar. Best part is the price it’s the most budget friendly LiFePo4 battery.”

The honest catch is the size of the battery: 128Wh is phone, router, laptop, and lights territory. It will run a typical 60W laptop for about two hours, and it will not run a CPAP overnight or cook a meal. And the price catch is real — $139.99 at Walmart against $119 at Amazon. If you are buying on price alone, Amazon wins by $20.99; if you are buying on Walmart, you are paying for the convenience, not the deal.

Who it’s for

Travelers, campers, and desk workers who want a grab-and-go station for the small stuff — phones, a laptop, a router, lights, a camera battery — and a UPS that fits on a shelf. At 3.97 lb it is the station you actually carry. It is not for anyone who needs a fridge, a CPAP through the night, or a whole-room backup: 128Wh is the wrong battery for that job, and the bigger sibling — the BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 at $259 — is the step up, when it is back in stock.

Performance in real use

The buyer evidence is two written reviews, both 5 stars. Imran (5 stars) is the substantive one: “This is the best most compact solar generator I have seen. Looks so cute and carrying it is so easy. Charges all our phones quickly. This even has a light bar. Best part is the price it’s the most budget friendly LiFePo4 battery.” The other is a short 5-star note — “Last of unit before upgrade. Love it.” No buyer has written about an outage run or a failure, so there is no load-test story to quote — the performance case rests on the spec sheet: 200W continuous (300W surge, 400W Power Lifting for resistive loads only) through one AC outlet, a 100W USB-C port for a laptop, two 15W USB-A ports, a second 15W USB-C port, and a 60W DC5521 port — six outputs in all. A 13-inch MacBook Air charges about 1.5 times from the 128Wh pack, per BLUETTI’s own figures.

Setup

Charge it before the first use — 0–80% in about 45 minutes on the 150W AC input, full in about 70 minutes. Solar tops out at 100W, car charging at 100W, and AC plus solar together cap at 150W. For UPS use, plug the protected load into the AC outlet and the station into the wall; switchover is 10 milliseconds. The Bluetooth app handles monitoring and the DC5521 voltage setting, and the built-in LED light has cold, warm, and SOS modes. At 128Wh, BLUETTI markets it as flight-approved — but it is above the 100Wh no-approval carry-on limit, so check your airline’s rules before flying with it.
