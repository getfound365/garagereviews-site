Who it's for

Campers and RVers who need quiet, clean power for sensitive electronics, and homeowners covering a fridge, freezer, and chargers in an outage. Not for: running a 13.5k BTU RV air conditioner alone, or whole-home backup — 2,200 running watts is a light-load machine (parallel-ready if you need more).

Performance in real use

Buyers in the newest 25 reviews report one-pull starts and quiet running. “Great little generator, starts with one pull, and is quiet,” wrote LTD77 (Nov 16, 2026). Another buyer completing the break-in period reported the unit “starts easily and runs fine” on gasoline while running a small space heater (David M., Nov 13, 2026) — though note that review is titled for the iGen2800DFc dual-fuel model, a listing-family sibling, not this exact unit. “Quiet” appears in 4 of the newest 25 and “easy” in 5. No buyer in the newest 25 reports a failure, a no-start, or a noise complaint.

Setup

Per the manual: add SAE 10W-30 oil (0.37 qt / 11.8 oz), add unleaded gasoline 87–93 octane (up to 10% ethanol; no E15/E85), and recoil-start. The LED display shows runtime, remaining fuel, load/output, volts, and lifetime hours. Break-in guidance mirrors the WGen3600: keep loads modest in the first hours. Buyers describe setup as quick and the display as useful.

How it compares

Against the A-iPower GXS4300i at $599.99, the iGen2500 gives up 1,250 running watts but is lighter and quieter on paper (52 dBA, 47.4 lb) with a far deeper review pool — and the GXS4300i is in stock while this one is not. Against the Aceup Energy AI4000S at $379.99, it is the quieter, better-reviewed, known-brand pick at a higher price and fewer watts. See the full side-by-side on the comparison page.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts is the iGen2500?

2,500 peak watts and 2,200 running watts, per the Westinghouse manual.

How quiet is the iGen2500?

Westinghouse rates it as low as 52 dBA. Buyers in the newest 25 reviews repeatedly describe it as quiet. We have not measured it ourselves.

Can it power sensitive electronics?

Yes — it is an inverter generator with ≤ 3% total harmonic distortion, per the manual, which is the standard threshold for laptops, phones, and TVs.

Is it parallel-ready?

Yes — it can be connected to a second compatible Westinghouse generator for more power (parallel kit sold separately; kit details unverified).

How much does the iGen2500 cost?

$450 at Walmart, but out of stock as of Nov 19, 2026. The eBay iGen2800 second option ($344.99) sold out Nov 25, 2026 — no buyable option right now.

Wasn't there a recall on the iGen2500?

Yes, historical only: a 2017 CPSC recall (18-055) covered about 4,500 iGen2500/iPro2500 units sold June–October 2017 for overheating/fire risk. It does not apply to the current listing.
