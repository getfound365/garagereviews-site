The verdict

For what it is — a 10 to 15 yard backyard target bow with a magazine nobody else has — $103.13 is a fair price, and the 4.2 stars from 109 ratings back it up. The three-shot magazine is the whole pitch: load three bolts, shoot, and the next one feeds itself. Every other pistol crossbow at this price makes you reload by hand, one at a time, and at 180 feet per second, three quick shots is what makes backyard plinking actually fun instead of a chore.

Two honest corrections to the video. First, the video's title says $99 — that was the price at filming. It has since moved to $103.13 — still the cheapest place to get it against Midway USA at $116.99. Second, and more important: this is not a hunting crossbow. Barnett doesn't recommend it for any game, and plenty of buyers will think it's a deer crossbow for about a hundred bucks. They're not. And never dry-fire it — releasing the string with no bolt loaded sends all the energy back into the string and the limbs, and you will damage both.

Who it's for

Anyone who wants a backyard target bow that's actually fun to shoot — paper and cans at 10 to 15 yards, three quick shots with no reloading between them. It is not for hunting: Barnett doesn't recommend it for any game. And it is not for range past 15 yards — past 20, the bolt drops fast.

Performance in real use

A recurve pistol crossbow you hold in one hand: 13.3 inches tip-to-tip cocked, 6-inch power stroke, 50 pounds of draw weight per Barnett's launch release, about 2 pounds. The patented three-shot magazine is an industry first, per Barnett — load three bolts and the next one feeds itself. Cocking is a single-stage break action: fold the front down, the string catches, close it back up, with a cocking release lever and a soft-touch thumb grip. The safety is automatic — it engages on every reload and locks the trigger until you switch it off yourself, which matters when your offhand sits right next to the string. Aiming is adjustable fiber-optic sights on a Picatinny rail, red and green pins you can actually see in daylight, plus a flip-down tactical foregrip: palm it like a pistol, or flip it down and hold it AR-style with two hands.

Setup

Break the action open one stage, the string catches, close it back up — and the safety clicks on by itself every time you reload. Load the bolt first, every single time, before your finger ever touches the trigger: safety on, bolt in, then shoot. Use the 7-inch Hyperbolt 7-2F, 2-fletch bolts — 5 come in the box, and they are exclusive to this bow. Here's the trap: the Accustrike, the Tri Strike's cheaper cousin, takes a 7-3F bolt, 3-fletch. It looks almost identical. It will not feed in this magazine. Buy the wrong pack and you've got bolts that don't fit and a magazine that won't cycle. The one thing it doesn't come with is the retractable sling — Barnett sells that separately.
