Who it's for

Whole-home backup. If your outage plan is the whole house — well pump, air conditioning, and everything else at once — through a transfer switch, this is the model in the roster built for it. It is not the portable pick; at 211 lb it lives where you place it.

Performance in real use

12,500 peak / 9,500 running watts on gas, 11,200 peak / 8,500 running watts on propane — the most running watts per dollar we list. Up to 12 hours of runtime from a 457cc engine and a 6.6-gallon tank. Output is 120/240V through an L14-30R transfer-switch outlet, with remote start.

What to know first

Two things to plan around. First, the weight: 211 lb needs two people or a ramp to load and place. Second, this is an open-frame design, so expect more noise than the inverter models — the listing gives no dBA figure, so none is quoted here. And for whole-home use it needs a transfer switch installed by an electrician. Never send generator power into a wall outlet.

How it compares

Against the iGen4500DF at $949, the WGen9500DF is the power pick: 9,500 running watts against 3,700, for $50 more — but the iGen is the portable, quiet one at 52 dBA and 98 lb, and it is the one with a video review. Against the iGen4000DFc at $799, it is a different class entirely: whole-home versus outage essentials.
