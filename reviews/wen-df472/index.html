Who it's for

Budget homeowners who want dual fuel and 240V output for a well pump, furnace blower, fridge, and lights, without paying $749 for the G12KBN. Not for: anyone who needs electric start, a CO sensor, or a deep review pool to lean on — this model has none of the three.

Performance in real use

The buyer record is small but mostly positive: 7 of the 9 Bazaarvoice reviews are 5-star. “It done it’s job well,” wrote William (3-star), running it on a deep well. The one 1-star review is a buyer whose unit didn’t start and was returned (Andy). With only 9 ratings total, one bad unit moves the average a lot — the 4.3-star figure should be read as “early and encouraging” rather than proven. No buyer reports a noise or runtime problem in the reviews we have.

Setup

Per WEN: add SAE 10W-30 oil, add unleaded gasoline or connect a propane tank with the included hose, and recoil-start. The fuel gauge shows the 4-gallon tank level, and the AVR keeps voltage steady. The 120/240V 30-amp L14-30R outlet connects to a transfer switch (such as the Reliance 310CRK) for house circuits. Note the neutral is bonded to the frame — check your transfer-switch requirements before connecting.

How it compares

Against the WEN 56475 at $525, the DF472 is the better value: dual fuel instead of gas-only, 120/240V instead of 120V only, and $25 cheaper — but it gives up electric start (recoil only) and has a far thinner review pool (9 ratings vs 137). Against the Pulsar G12KBN at $749, it is a smaller, simpler machine: a third of the running watts, no 50-amp outlet, at two-thirds the price — and neither model lists a CO sensor.
