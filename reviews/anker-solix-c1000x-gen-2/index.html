Who it's for

Anyone who wants silent indoor-safe backup for a fridge, a CPAP, or a home office through the first hours of an outage — and who will use it within its 2,000-watt limit. Skip it if you need to run a well pump, a space heater, or a whole fridge for a full day: 1,024Wh is a few hours of real load, not a day. Skip it if the surge-number conflict bothers you — the EcoFlow DELTA 3 at $438.90 below has a clean 1,800W / 3,600W surge spec with no ambiguity.

Performance in real use

The LiFePO4 battery is rated for 4,000+ cycles — years of nightly use, not a season. Continuous output is 2,000 watts, and the station recharges 0–100% in 49 minutes from the wall, per Anker. At 27.12 pounds it is a one-hand carry to the campsite or the bedroom. The review base is thin — 55 ratings, 33 written reviews — so treat the 4.5-star figure as early evidence, not a verdict from thousands of owners. What buyers do say, in the Bazaarvoice sample, is consistent with Anker's fast-charge claim: this is a station bought for speed and capacity, not for running a whole house.

Setup

Charge it to 100% before the first trip — 49 minutes from the wall — and leave it between 20% and 80% in storage if you will not use it for months. The display shows the remaining runtime; the app shows the same. Solar charging takes a panel (sold separately); the Walmart listing is the station alone. Keep it dry and indoors: this is a battery, not a generator, and it belongs inside the house during an outage, not in the rain. And check the model number A1765111 on the box when it arrives — see the note above.
