Who it's for

Campers, CPAP users, and anyone who wants home backup for the essentials — fridge, laptop, lights, phones — and who accepts 22.5 lb and a variant page that needs a careful read. Skip it if you need to run high-wattage appliances, if you want the lightest station per watt-hour, or if the shared listing’s capacity picker makes you nervous — pick the 10,048 mAh option and confirm it reads AC70.

Performance in real use

The headline numbers hold up across BLUETTI’s own page and the Walmart listing: 768Wh, 1,000W rated output, 2,000W lifting power for pure-resistive loads like space heaters, hair dryers, and kettles. Charging is the strong suit — 850W AC input with a Turbo mode that fills it in 1.4–1.5 hours, up to 500W of solar input (BLUETTI recommends one PV350 or two PV200 panels), plus car and lead-acid charging. Seven outlets cover AC, USB-A, USB-C, and 12V DC, and the BLUETTI app controls it over Bluetooth, including the Silent, Standard, and Turbo charging modes — Silent runs at 45 dB.

In real use, buyers report exactly the use case this station is built for: overnight CPAP, outdoor equipment, and outage backup, with fast recharges between uses. The recurring knocks are weight for the capacity, the sense that 1,000W is not enough for bigger loads, and one damaged-box shipping story that turned into a hard return. The LiFePO4 chemistry is the long-game argument: 3,000+ cycles to 80% capacity and a 10-year expected lifespan, backed by a 5-year warranty.

Setup

Charge it to full first, then pick a charging mode in the BLUETTI app — Silent (45 dB), Standard, or Turbo for the fastest refill. For solar, use panels with 12–58V open-circuit voltage and MC4 connectors (one PV350 or two PV200, per BLUETTI). Keep it upright in storage and in use, and don’t recharge it below freezing — BLUETTI’s charging range is 32–104 °F, while discharging works down to −4 °F.
