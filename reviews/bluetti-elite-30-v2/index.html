The verdict

On the specs, this is the small station to beat: 288Wh of LiFePO4, a 600W pure sine wave inverter, a 140W USB-C port that fast-charges a laptop, a 10-millisecond UPS, and a 45-minute 0–80% recharge — all at 9.48 lb, about a third of the weight of the 768Wh BLUETTI AC70. It was the named Black Friday pick in both Bob Vila and Field & Stream, which is why it sits at the top of today’s list.

The honest catch is the one that matters most: you cannot buy it right now. The Light Sand Grey variant is out of stock at Walmart at $259, and the other variants’ stock is unconfirmed, so this page is a watch-and-wait, not a buy. If you need a station today, the smaller sibling — the BLUETTI Elite 10 at $139.99 — is in stock, and the Pecron F1000LFP at $369 is the in-stock step up to a 1kWh-class battery. If you can wait, this is the one to watch for a restock.

Who it’s for

Anyone who wants a grab-and-go station for a desk, a campsite, or a short outage: a router, a laptop, phones, lights, a fan, a TV. At 9.48 lb it carries one-handed, and the 10ms UPS means it can sit behind a router or computer full-time. It is not for anyone who needs a fridge through the night — 288Wh is roughly a router for most of a day, not a refrigerator — and it is not for heating loads, despite the 1,500W Power Lifting headline: that figure is resistive-only, at reduced power.

Performance in real use

The buyer evidence is thin and that is worth saying plainly: 67 ratings at 4.2 stars on Walmart, but only one written review in Bazaarvoice — a 5-star ratings-only review from rhonda, with no text. There is no buyer-written account of a fridge run or an outage to quote, so this section leans on the spec sheet and the independent reviews that exist (TechRadar, MacRumors, Gadget Flow, and BatterySkills all published dedicated reviews of this model). The consistent picture from those reviews: 600W covers laptops, monitors, routers, fans, lights, a TV, and camera or drone batteries; 288Wh is a small-loads battery, and the 200W solar cap is the weak spot — a 60W panel takes about 5.5 hours, per BLUETTI. Nine outputs share the 600W total, so the AC outlets and the USB-C ports draw from the same budget.

Setup

Charge it before the first use — 0–80% in about 45 minutes on the 380W AC input, full in about 70 minutes. Solar tops out at 200W through an XT60 port (12–28V), good for about 1.7 hours in strong sun. For UPS use, plug the protected load into the AC outlets and the station into the wall; switchover is 10 milliseconds or less with a 980W pass-through. The app runs over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. It is rated IP20 — indoor, dry use only.
