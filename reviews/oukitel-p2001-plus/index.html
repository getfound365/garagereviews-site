The verdict

Buy it at $659.99 if you want a full-size home-backup station and you are comfortable with a thin buyer record: 2,048Wh of LiFePO4, a 2,400W pure sine wave inverter with a 4,800W surge, thirteen outputs, and an 1,800W AC input that gets it to 80% in about an hour. That is the same battery and the same inverter as the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max class, for hundreds less — and the cycle rating is 3,500+ to 80%, ahead of EcoFlow’s 3,000.

The honest catch is trust, not specs. Five ratings on Walmart, two reviews in Bazaarvoice, both 5 stars — and one of them is Matt, who wrote “Glad I bought it for upcoming ice storm” and admitted he had not had need to use it yet. There is no buyer-written account of a fridge run or a full outage to quote. Add the FFpower brand-field mismatch, and this is a buy-the-specs pick, not a buy-the-crowd pick. If that bothers you, the Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 at $1,349 is the same 2kWh class with a 300-rating base and a brand with a longer track record — for $689 more.

Who it’s for

Homeowners who want a fridge, a microwave, power tools, and the essentials covered through an outage, and RVers or campers who need a big battery that recharges fast. At 48.5 lb it is luggable — a pantry shelf or the back of an SUV — not a one-handed carry. It is not for anyone who wants the battery to grow: 2,048Wh is fixed, with no expansion port. And it is not for solar-first setups: the 500W solar cap is half what the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max takes.

Performance in real use

The two written reviews are both 5 stars and both short. Matt (5 stars) bought it ahead of an ice storm and wrote, “I haven’t had need to use it yet but this coming ice storm will put it on trial.” The other review is a ratings-only 5 stars from arlety. That is the whole buyer record — no heavy-load test, no outage story, no failure report. What the spec sheet says it can do: 2,400W continuous covers a fridge, a microwave, or a 15-amp power tool, and the 4,800W surge covers motor starts. Four AC outlets, four USB-C ports (100W, 45W, and two 20W), two 18W USB-A, a 12V car socket, and two DC barrel ports — thirteen outputs in all, the most USB-C ports in the 2kWh class. The EPS switchover is under 10 milliseconds, fast enough to hold a computer or router through a blip.

Setup

Charge it before the first use — 0–80% in about 1 hour on the 1,800W AC input (adjustable in 400W steps), full in about 1.5 hours. Solar tops out at 500W through an XT60 port (12–60V, 15A), and AC plus solar together reach 2,300W for a 50-minute 0–80% charge. For backup use, plug the protected load into the AC outlets and the station into the wall; the EPS switches in under 10 milliseconds. There is no expansion battery to wire in — 2,048Wh is the ceiling.
