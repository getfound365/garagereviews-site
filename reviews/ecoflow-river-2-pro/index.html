The verdict

At $349 it was $200 off the $549 list — but it is out of stock at Walmart as of Nov 21, 2026. If it comes back, it is the deal of this batch for anyone who wants a real power station — not a phone charger with a handle — for camping, a CPAP overnight, or the first hours of an outage. Buyers give it 4.5 stars from 790 ratings, and the praise is specific: “powers my CPAP all night when camping off grid,” “high efficiency in easy to carry size.” One buyer recharges it during the day with a generator in about an hour.

The honest catches: this is the 716Wh variant, not the 768Wh flagship, and the price is a list-price markdown, not a new low — it has been $349 before. At 800 watts continuous it will run a fridge, a fan, and phones, but not a space heater or a well pump. If you need more, the DELTA 3 at $438.90 is the next step up.

Who it's for

Anyone who camps off-grid, runs a CPAP overnight, or wants silent indoor-safe backup for the first hours of an outage — and who will use it within its 800-watt limit. Skip it if you need to run a well pump, a space heater, or a whole fridge for a full day: 716Wh is a few hours of real load, not a day. Skip it if the 716Wh-vs-768Wh variant question bothers you — buy the 768Wh flagship direct from EcoFlow instead, at the same $349.

Performance in real use

The LiFePO4 battery is rated for 3,000+ cycles to 80% capacity — years of nightly use, not a season. AC output is 800 watts continuous with a 1,600-watt surge (X-Boost), and it recharges 0–100% in 70 minutes from the wall. The under-30ms EPS switchover means a plugged-in CPAP or router keeps running through a blip. At 18.2 pounds it is a one-hand carry to the campsite or the bedroom. Buyers report it holding a charge through a night of CPAP use and recharging fast the next day. The one-star reviews are mostly delivery problems — “never got it,” “wouldn't hold charge” — not performance complaints from owners who received a working unit.

Setup

Charge it to 100% before the first trip — 70 minutes from the wall — and leave it between 20% and 80% in storage if you will not use it for months. The app (EcoFlow's) shows the remaining runtime; the display on the unit shows the same. Solar charging takes a panel (sold separately); the Walmart listing is the station alone. Keep it dry and indoors: this is a battery, not a generator, and it belongs inside the house during an outage, not in the rain.
