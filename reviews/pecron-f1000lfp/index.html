The verdict

Buy it at $369 if you want the cheapest honest 1kWh-class station in the roster: 960Wh, a 1,500W pure sine wave inverter, a sub-20ms UPS, and a 1,000W AC input that gets it to 80% in 50 minutes. Buyers give it 4.3 stars from 14 ratings, and the Bazaarvoice cross-check is 4.33 from 9 reviews — close enough that the live stats call stays on. The buyer evidence is small but concrete: Tara (5 stars) ran a refrigerator for six hours during a scheduled outage and charged two phones and a tablet, with no issues.

The honest catch is the one 1-star review: Dustin’s unit worked the first day camping, then “completely let us down” on the second morning. One failure in a nine-review base is not a pattern, but it is the reason to buy from a seller with a real return path and to test the unit on arrival — and the reason the 3,500-cycle LFP battery and 5-year warranty matter more here than the headline price.

Who it’s for

Car campers, RVers, and homeowners who want a short-outage station for the essentials — a router, lights, phones, a CPAP, and a few hours of refrigerator — and are willing to manage the load. At 1,500W it covers a coffee maker or small microwave, which 700W stations cannot. It is not for anyone who wants a station that grows: the battery is fixed at 960Wh, and there is no compatible expansion battery.

Performance in real use

The six text reviews split cleanly. The good: Tara’s six-hour fridge run during an outage, Christine (4 stars) praising the built-in light during power outages, George (5 stars) saying “for the price, this little guy beats the competition,” and MomofThree30107 (5 stars) buying it as a second brand after owning several Jackery stations, in case something goes wrong with one brand. The bad: Dustin’s day-two failure on a camping trip. The 25 dB rating applies under a 500W load — near-silent in a bedroom or office — and the 600W solar input (XT60, 11–60V) recharges it in about 2 hours of good sun. One independent tester measured roughly 80% inverter efficiency at rated output, so the 960Wh pack delivers less than 960Wh to an AC appliance — budget for about 750Wh of usable AC power.

Setup

Charge it before the first use — 0–80% in 50 minutes on the wall, full in about 1.1 hours. The front panel carries all ten outputs on one face: four AC outlets, two USB-C (100W and 33W), USB-A, a 12V car socket, and a DC 5525 port, plus the 5W LED light. The app (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth) is optional. For UPS use, plug the protected load into the AC outlets and the station into the wall; switchover is under 20 milliseconds, fast enough for a computer or router. For storage, Pecron’s rule is stricter than most: fully charge it at least once every two months, and for long-term storage charge it fully, then discharge to 60% every three months.
