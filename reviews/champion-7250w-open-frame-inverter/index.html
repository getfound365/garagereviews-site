The verdict

The Champion 7250W is the quieter big-unit play at $999: an open-frame inverter that makes 5,500 running watts with clean power (under 3% THD) for sensitive electronics, plus a 120/240V locking outlet so it can feed a transfer switch. At the same $999 as the whole-home WGen9500DF, it trades raw watts for inverter power and a lighter, quieter frame — the right trade if your loads include electronics and you do not need 9,500 running watts.

The honest catch is the evidence: 6 ratings is barely a review base, so the 4.5-star figure is a weak signal and this page leans on the spec sheet instead. It is also gas-only and recoil-start only — no propane, no push-button or remote start. If you want dual fuel or electric start at this price, look at the comparables below. And the storm rule applies: this is a Thanksgiving-week outage buy, so run it outdoors, at least 20 feet from the house, exhaust pointing away, never in a garage.

Who it’s for

Anyone who needs more than the outage essentials — a fridge, lights, furnace fan, and a well pump or a window AC at once — and wants clean inverter power for electronics while they are at it. The RV outlet and the locking 120/240V outlet suit RVers and anyone wiring a transfer switch. It is not for anyone who wants propane as a second fuel, or push-button start, or a quiet enclosed inverter — at 69 dBA and 111.6 lb, this is a big open-frame unit that you hear and plan around.

Performance in real use

5,500 running watts on gas covers a fridge, lights, furnace fan, phones, and a well pump or a mid-size AC at once, with under 3% THD for laptops and TVs. The 4.71-gallon tank runs up to 15 hours at 25% load. At 69 dBA from 23 feet it is louder than an enclosed inverter — Champion’s own claim is 50% quieter than a traditional 5,500-watt generator, not silent — and the buyer evidence agrees it is reasonably quiet for its size: Rogue Ridge, who ran a woodworking business through a fire-season shutoff, wrote in June 2026 that it was “easy to start, easy to operate, and noise levels were reasonably low” and kept several pieces of equipment running at once. M-A LaFleur bought one after an ice storm and wrote, “I bought this after the ice storm and feel totally ready for the next weather event.”

Setup

Fill the 4.71-gallon tank with gas, check the 0.8-quart oil (10W-30 included), and pull the recoil start — Cold Start Technology is built in, but there is no electric or remote start on this model. Then the hard rule: at least 20 feet from the house, exhaust pointing away, never in a garage or under a porch roof. CO Shield shuts the unit down if carbon monoxide builds up around it, but the shutoff is a backup, not a license to run it close — keep a battery CO detector inside the house as well.

How it compares

Against the Westinghouse WGen9500DF at $999, the Champion 7250W gives up whole-home power — 9,500 running watts and a transfer-switch outlet — but it is an inverter, so its power is clean enough for sensitive electronics, and it is quieter and lighter at 111.6 lb against 211 lb. Against the Champion 201319 4500W Dual Fuel Inverter at $999, it gives up dual fuel entirely — gas only — but gains 1,000 more running watts and the 120/240V locking outlet. Frequently asked questions

How many watts does the Champion 7250W make?

7,250 peak watts and 5,500 running watts on gasoline. It is gas-only — there is no propane option on this model.

How loud is it?

69 dBA at 23 feet, per Champion. That is louder than an enclosed inverter but about half as loud as a traditional open-frame generator of the same size, per Champion’s own comparison.

Does it have a CO shutoff?

Yes — CO Shield carbon monoxide auto shutoff is built in, and the unit shuts itself down if carbon monoxide builds up around it.

Can it run an RV?

Yes — it is RV Ready with a 120V 30A TT-30R RV outlet, plus a 120/240V locking outlet for a transfer switch.

How long will it run?

Up to 15 hours on a tank of gas at 25% load, from the 4.71-gallon tank.
