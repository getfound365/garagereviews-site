The verdict

Buy it at $949 if you want the most generator for the money in the roster: 7,500 running watts on gas, dual fuel, remote start, and a transfer-switch-ready 30-amp outlet, backed by 6,058 buyer ratings at 4.7 stars — the largest review base we have ever picked. The Bazaarvoice cross-check is 4.74 stars from 6,110 reviews, which tracks the Walmart figure closely, so the live stats call stays on.

The honest catch is what it is not: an inverter. At 72 dBA it is about as loud as a dishwasher, total harmonic distortion is ≤23%, and at 202.1 lb dry it is a wheeled unit, not a carry unit. If your loads are a fridge, furnace fan, lights, and tools through a transfer switch, this is the pick. If your loads are laptops, a CPAP, and anything with a circuit board, buy the inverter instead.

Who it’s for

Homeowners who want whole-home backup through a manual transfer switch and are happy to run it on gas or propane. It is transfer-switch ready with a 120/240V 30-amp twist-lock outlet, so an electrician can wire it to a manual transfer switch for essentials like the fridge, furnace fan, and lights. It is not an inverter, so check sensitive electronics with their makers first — total harmonic distortion is ≤23%. It is not for anyone who needs to carry a generator: at 202.1 lb dry, it moves on its wheels or it does not move.

Setup

Plan on about 30 minutes: mount the wheel kit and feet, add the included oil (1.16 quart, SAE 10W-30), add gas, and connect the battery for the electric start. Starting is recoil, electric push-button, or the included remote key fob. The control panel carries a VFT display (voltage, frequency, lifetime hours) and a built-in fuel gauge on the 6.6-gallon tank. For home backup, have an electrician wire the 120/240V 30-amp outlet to a manual transfer switch — never backfeed through a household outlet. Run it outdoors only, far from windows, doors, and vents: this is an open-frame generator with a CO sensor that shuts it down, not an indoor appliance.
