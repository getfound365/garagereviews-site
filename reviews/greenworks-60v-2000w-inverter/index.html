The verdict

Buy it at $748 only if you already own Greenworks 60V batteries — two or more, ideally. Then it is a genuinely useful box: a 2,000W pure sine wave inverter, three AC outlets, two 100W USB-C ports, a smart LCD, and a 1,500W charger that refills four 4Ah batteries in 0.6 hours. It turns battery packs that would otherwise sit on a shelf into a backup station for the fridge, the jobsite, or a week off-grid.

Do not buy it as your first power station. Without batteries it does, in the words of one 1-star buyer, “absolutely nothing” — and the buyers split exactly on that line. Steve (5 stars) calls it a “really useful addition if you have multiple 60v tools” that “makes a nice portable power and charging station out of my existing lawn tools.” Mark (3 stars) is blunter: “it’s a neat idea to have to use your batteries in another way for backup power. but for the price you can get a power station.” Frank (1 star) put it hardest: “$800! for a charger! without batteries it does absolutely nothing. Batteries not included… that’s another $800!” Frank’s $800 reflects an earlier price — today’s is $748 — but his point stands: price this against what you already own, not against a standalone station.

Who it’s for

Greenworks 60V owners — mower, blower, trimmer, and tool owners with two to four batteries already in the garage. For them it is a second life for packs they own, a jobsite power source, a camping station, and a storm backup in one box. It is not for anyone starting from zero: if you own no 60V batteries, a complete station costs less than this empty box, and the batteries to fill it would cost hundreds more.

Performance in real use

Nineteen written reviews in Bazaarvoice, 4.26 stars — the largest review base in today’s power-station set — and they split into two camps. The owners’ camp: Hank256 (5 stars) calls it “a must have for power back up for the home, especially during storms” and likes that “the batteries can charge in the power station and be ready to go when needed”; Ollie’s Mom (5 stars) ran Starlink, laptops, and phones on a week-long camping trip; Mollie (5 stars) ran a small electric grill and a Keurig on a scout camping trip with two batteries; aec38 (5 stars) plugged in a freezer and tracked battery life from the app. The skeptics’ camp: the price, and the missing batteries — Mark’s 3-star “for the price you can get a power station” and Frank’s 1-star charger complaint. One 1-star review, from Rboi, says the unit arrived defective and does not charge — the only defect report in the set. On the spec sheet, 2,000W continuous covers a refrigerator, a TV, a coffee maker, or AC power tools, and the 3,000W surge covers motor starts.

Setup

Slot in charged Greenworks 60V batteries — any of them, in any of the four slots — and plug in. There is no fuel, no pull cord, no warm-up. The smart LCD shows output power, remaining time, and AC/DC status, and the SMARTg app adds Bluetooth monitoring. Plugged into the wall, the unit doubles as a charger: the built-in 1,500W fast charger refills four 4Ah batteries in 0.6 hours. The LED light covers low-light use, and the dual-handle design is built for carrying — though Greenworks does not publish a weight for this unit, so we do not list one.
