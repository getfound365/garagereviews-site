The verdict

The DCK225D2 is the compact way into DeWalt’s 20V MAX system: a drill/driver that fits where a full-size drill cannot, an impact driver with real 1,700 in-lb torque, and two batteries so one can charge while the other works. Buyers rate it 4.6 stars from 226 ratings, and the recurring theme in the reviews is that it replaces a much older, heavier drill without giving up power — exactly the job a combo kit is for.

The honest catch is the price picture, not the tools. Walmart.com has no offer on this kit at all, so the only way to buy it through Walmart is a third-party seller at $188.08 — and Home Depot has the same kit at $169.00 for Black Friday. If you are buying today, check Home Depot’s $169 before you pay $188.08 to a marketplace seller, and note the 2.0Ah batteries: fine for drilling and driving around the house, small for all-day work.

Who it’s for

Anyone building or replacing a cordless drill setup around the house or the garage — drilling pilot holes, driving screws, assembling, hanging, and light repair work in tight spaces where a full-size drill will not fit. The 5.88-inch drill head and 5.1-inch impact driver are the point: cabinets, plumbing, electrical rough-in, and punch-list work. It is not for anyone who needs all-day runtime from the included batteries — 2.0Ah packs are small, and heavy users will want a 4.0Ah or 5.0Ah pack — and it is not a hammer-drill or high-torque impact wrench replacement.

Performance in real use

The DCD794 drill/driver makes 404 unit watts out with up to 1,650 RPM no-load, a 1/2-inch ratcheting chuck, and a 2-speed transmission — DeWalt rates it up to 16% more power than the older DCD771 on the same battery. The DCF809 impact driver delivers 1,700 in-lbs of torque at up to 2,800 RPM, with a variable-speed trigger for control on delicate surfaces. Buyer evidence backs the compact claim: Ghedd, an electrician, wrote in November 2026 that the kit replaced a 25-year-old DeWalt drill and that he was happy with “how much lighter this is than previous generation,” adding “Great power so far. Used both tools so far. Like a lot.” tom wrote the same month that the “tools are great” and the “battery seem to hold up really well.” The one 1-star review in the newest batch is a delivery complaint — “Never received” — not a complaint about the tools.

Setup

Charge both DCB203 2.0Ah batteries, clip a belt hook on each tool, and pick the right one for the job: the drill/driver for holes and precise driving, the impact driver for screws and fasteners that need torque. The drill’s 15 clutch settings and LED light cover delicate work; the impact driver’s three LEDs light the fastener, not the wall. Both tools run on the same 20V MAX batteries as the rest of DeWalt’s 20V MAX line, so a bigger pack from another DeWalt tool fits straight on.
