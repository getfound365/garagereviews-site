The verdict

$99 for two cellular cameras that do the job hunters actually buy them for: 40-megapixel buck photos, a half-second trigger, and a connection that finds signal where your phone can't. With 4.4 stars on the 2-pack and a bestseller badge, this is the cheapest way into the Edge lineup — the Edge 2 is $53.97 for one camera, the Edge 2 Pro $65.48 — and the pair saves about $41 against two singles at $69.99. Two field tests back the headline: a month in Colorado and six months on a 120-acre lease.

But the sticker price is only half the story. Each camera needs its own $9.99-a-month plan — about $20 a month for the pair, every month you run them, roughly two and a half times the cost of the cameras themselves in a year. Buy it if you hunt remote ground — a lease, a food plot, a feeder you can't visit every week — and you want buck photos on your phone without walking in. Skip it if you only hunt one small property within walking distance: a $60 card camera does that job without a subscription. And skip it if the plan cost bothers you, because $9.99 a month per camera, every month, is the real price of this camera.

Who it's for

Hunters who need to monitor remote areas — a lease, a food plot, a feeder — and want buck photos on their phone without physically visiting the location. It suits anyone who wants a simple setup and a reliable connection. If you only hunt one small property within walking distance, a $60 card camera is the better buy — no subscription, no monthly cost.

Performance in real use

The image and the trigger are the two things that decide whether you actually see the buck, and the field tests back them both. 40 megapixels, 1080p video, a half-second trigger, 100 feet of detection and flash range. In Colorado, the reviewer ran his fast black lab past the camera at 50 feet — centered in every photo, trigger a little better than half a second, detection still picking up the dog at 105 feet. The six-month tester on a 120-acre lease says he can zoom into a buck at 60 feet and count the antler points. The low-glow flash is barely noticeable, and neither tester spooked a deer. The honest limit: night video gets grainy past about 50 feet.

Setup

Scan a code, and it connects. No SIM card, no SD card — built-in memory stores everything in the app, and the camera auto-connects to the strongest of four major U.S. carriers. There is no screen on the camera; everything happens on your phone. Each camera comes with a heavy-duty strap and a two-year warranty from the day you activate it in the Moultrie app, plus three free months of OnX Hunt Elite. Batteries, solar, and the plan are separate. The mistake most hunters make is leaving the PIR sensitivity on medium or high through a hot, windy summer — every gust of hot grass trips the sensor, hundreds of false triggers, dead batteries, burned data. There are five PIR settings, lowest to highest: hot and windy, drop to low or lowest, and turn on smart capture, buck only. And push the solar power jack in until it seats — one tester didn't, and his panel went dead.
