The verdict

Buy it for the UPS job. The HomePower 1000 v2 is built to keep a home office alive through a blip: 1,024Wh of LiFePO4, 1,500W continuous (3,000W surge), and a sub-10ms switchover that Backup Power Hub tested as a real UPS for routers, PCs, and CPAP machines. Jackery rates the battery for 6,000 cycles to 70%+ capacity, and advertises an Emergency Charge in 50 minutes; SolarLab's independent test measured 0–100% in about 1 hour 10 minutes at 87% efficiency. Priority Routing — Jackery's feature that lets you choose which outputs stay live as the battery drains — is the kind of thing that matters at 2 a.m. in an outage, and SolarLab also tested charging the unit from a standard gas generator.

The honest limits are capacity and track record. At 1,024Wh fixed with no expansion port, Backup Power Hub puts it at about 6 hours on a full-size fridge, and one jackery.com buyer reports it could not power a freezer long-term. And there is no owner rating on the Walmart listing yet — no stars, no written reviews — so the verdict here rests on the spec sheet and two independent reviews, not on hundreds of buyers. At $469 it is the cheapest way into the 1kWh LiFePO4 class today; if you need a whole-home battery or room to grow, step up to the 2,048Wh class instead.

Who it's for

Home-office workers and outage planners who need a router, PC, CPAP, or fridge to survive a blip or a short outage, and who want a UPS that outlasts a traditional battery backup. Skip it if you need whole-home power or expansion — 1,024Wh is fixed, with no way to add capacity later — and skip it if you need an owner track record first: this Walmart listing has no ratings yet.

Performance in real use

On the spec sheet this is a mid-size home-backup station done right: 1,024Wh LiFePO4, 1,500W continuous / 3,000W surge pure sine wave, three AC outlets, USB-C at 100W and 30W, USB-A at 18W, a 12V 10A car socket, and a built-in light. The Walmart spec block lists a 20 Ah battery capacity and an 8-hour runtime (load not stated), and 6,000 cycles to 70%+ capacity. The two independent reviews agree on the substance: SolarLab measured 87% efficiency and a 0–100% recharge in about 1 hour 10 minutes, and liked Priority Routing and gas-generator charging; Backup Power Hub scored it 8.7/10 overall, with UPS performance at 9.3/10, and put the fridge runtime at about 6 hours. The limitation both reviews name is the same one: capacity is fixed at 1,024Wh with no expansion port.

Setup

Charge it from a wall outlet — Jackery advertises an Emergency Charge in 50 minutes — or from up to 400W of solar through two 200W input ports, per SolarLab's review. Plug the essentials into the three AC outlets (router, PC, CPAP, fridge) and leave it inline as a UPS: the sub-10ms switchover means the gear never notices the grid dropping. Priority Routing, in Jackery's own feature list, lets you decide which outputs get power first when the battery runs low — set that before the storm, not during it. No assembly, no fuel, no fumes: it is a battery, so it runs indoors.
