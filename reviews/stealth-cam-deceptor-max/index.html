The verdict

Who it's for

Anyone who wants two cellular cameras covering two trails or two properties for about $102, and who accepts the Command plan as part of the price. Skip it if you will not pay a subscription: without a Command plan, a cellular camera cannot send photos to your phone. Skip it if you need fast video — the 0.76-second measured video trigger will miss the first step of a walking deer. Skip it if native high resolution matters to you: the 40MP figure is interpolated, and a camera with a genuinely high native resolution costs more.

Performance in real use

Trailcampro measured the trigger at 0.14 seconds for photos and 0.76 seconds for videos — fast on photos, slow on video, and the honest split matters more than any single number. Detection range is 120 ft at a 37.7° detection angle with a 41.3° field of view, and the No-Glo flash keeps the camera invisible at night. Video is 1440p QHD with audio. Battery life on Energizer Ultimate Lithium AAs is 8.4 months on photos and 3.8 months on videos, per Trailcampro — and each camera takes 16 AAs, so budget 32 batteries for the pair plus spares. The Command app adds animal recognition, on-demand photos, and mapping. The 36-LED 940nm flash and dual-SIM AT&T/Verizon auto-select are listing-stated, not Trailcampro-measured, so treat them as the listing's claim.

Setup

Load 16 AA batteries per camera — lithium AAs last far longer than alkalines in the cold — and a Class 10 SD card up to 32GB in each. Activate a Command plan before you leave the camera in the woods: plans are $5, $8, or $20 a month ($48, $72, or $120 a year), and each camera needs its own plan. The 12V external battery jack is the route if you want to stop changing AAs. Strap it to a tree at about knee-to-chest height, facing north if you can, and test it before you walk away: walk past it once and check that the photo lands on your phone.
