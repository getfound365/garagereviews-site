The verdict

Buy it at $116.98 if you want the established REVEAL ecosystem: 4K photos, 1080p video, a 0.5-second trigger, a 96 ft low-glow IR flash, multi-carrier LTE that picks the strongest signal, built-in GPS, and live aiming from the app. Buyers give it 4.3 stars from 273 ratings, and the Bazaarvoice cross-check is 4.31 from 111 reviews — a subset of the Walmart count, so this page shows the Walmart figure and the live stats call stays off.

The honest catch is batteries — it is the recurring theme in the newest reviews, three times over: “Eats batteries,” “Batteries last a week,” “Goes through batteries quickly.” Plan on lithium AAs or the rechargeable lithium cartridge, and consider the solar panel. The other catch is the plan: $5–$13 a month per camera, on top of the $116.98, or the camera sends nothing to your phone. One buyer, Kirk, put both halves in one line: “Great camera. Eats batteries. Definitely purchase the rechargeable lithium battery packs.”

Who it's for

Hunters and landowners who want proven cellular scouting with GPS, live aiming, and deer analytics — and who accept a $5–$13-a-month plan and a battery habit as part of the price. Skip it if you won’t pay a plan (without one, the camera cannot send photos), if you want solar in the box (that’s the SPYPOINT bundle’s pitch), or if a marketplace seller instead of Walmart.com bothers you.

Performance in real use

The sensor story needs one honest footnote: Walmart’s listing says 16MP images, while B&H lists an 8.3MP native sensor — the 4K output is upscaled, which is normal in this category and not a defect, but megapixels alone don’t tell you the image quality. What matters in the field is the rest of the sheet: a 0.5-second trigger (B&H lists 0.4s), a 96 ft low-glow IR flash at 850 nm, 1080p video in 10-, 15-, or 30-second clips, a 3-shot burst mode, and on-demand HD photo and video requests from the REVEAL app, plus live camera aiming so you can check the angle without walking back to the tree.

Connectivity is the reason to buy this generation: a preactivated integrated SIM that auto-connects to the strongest of the major carriers, cellular firmware updates, and an app with mapping, weather data, and deer analytics. It is IP66 rated and runs from −13 to 140 °F. The 1-star tail in the newest reviews is real, though — a unit that never worked well, one that took in water, and one that would not connect to the phone — so test yours in the yard before you hang it a mile from the truck.

Setup

Strap it to a tree at about knee-to-chest height, facing north if you can — sun glare is the enemy of every trail camera. Load 12 AA lithium batteries (the camera itself says to use lithium for best lifespan, and buyers who ran alkaline report a week of life), activate a REVEAL plan in the app before you leave it in the woods, and test it: walk past it once and check that the photo lands on your phone. The plan is per camera, so two cameras means two plans.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of the Tactacam Reveal X 3.0?

Do I need a data plan for the Reveal X 3.0?

Yes — a REVEAL plan is required to send photos to your phone: Starter $5 a month (250 photos), Intermediate $8 a month (500 photos), Pro $13 a month (unlimited), per Tactacam’s plan page. Without a plan the camera cannot send images over cellular.

Is the IR flash no-glow or low-glow?

Low-glow, with a 96 ft range. The no-glow flash belongs to other REVEAL models, not the X 3.0.

How fast is the trigger?

0.5 seconds per Tactacam’s retailer listings (B&H lists 0.4s) — sub-second either way.

Does the X 3.0 need an SD card?

No — it has 8GB of built-in storage, with an optional SD card slot for more storage.

How is the X 3.0 different from the X 2.0?

It is the newer generation — a pre-installed durable antenna, built-in GPS, and improved image sensors — for $36.99 more than the X 2.0 at $79.00.
