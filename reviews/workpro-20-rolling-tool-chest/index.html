Who it's for

Anyone setting up a first chest in a condo, apartment, or small garage who wants a real rolling cabinet at a sale price. Skip it if you need heavy-duty storage — the max load is not stated for this model, and the thin-metal reports on its bigger sibling say this is a light-duty family. Skip it if your tools need the 24.5-inch width — measure first, because several buyers were surprised by the size. And skip it if you cannot deal with a possible return: the damage and key reports in the newest reviews are worth planning around.

Performance in real use

The size is the story: assembled it stands 40.4 inches tall and 25.7 wide, with a top chest of 13.2 by 19.3 by 10 inches and a rolling base of 27.7 by 22 by 11.6 — big enough for a full set of hand tools, small enough for a closet or a condo garage. The top box detaches and works as a portable toolbox, and the bottom cabinet has doors for larger cases. The drawers run well when they work, and buyers call the chest sturdy for the price. The weak points are the ones to check on arrival: dents in shipping, a bottom drawer that does not work, and a top box that can sit so tight in the base that it is hard to separate the first time.

Setup

Attach the four casters and set the top box on the rolling base. Expect the top box to sit tight — one buyer could not get it out of the bottom at first and hoped it would loosen with use. Before you load it, find the keys and test the lock: two of the newest 25 reviews report a locked top box with the key missing or unreachable, and one buyer ended up drilling out the lock and replacing it. Open every drawer, check for dents, and keep the box until you are sure it is straight.
