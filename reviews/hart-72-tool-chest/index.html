Who it's for

Anyone who owns their home, has a large garage with six feet of wall space, and wants a single rolling workstation that combines a bench and a cabinet. Skip it if your garage is tight — 72 inches does not shrink — if your budget is closer to $500, or if you want drawer sizes, caster ratings, and lock details in writing first. The listing does not give you all of them. And skip it if you can wait: the October sale proves this cabinet goes on deep discount.

Performance in real use

Fifteen drawers in three banks: shallow drawers up top for hand tools — wrenches, sockets, screwdrivers, the stuff you grab every day — deeper drawers down low for power tools and bulky gear. The top is what makes it a workbench instead of just a cabinet: a solid wood top, 72 by 24 inches of real work surface — break down a carburetor on it, lay out a project, clamp something down. The listing does not publish the top's thickness or the wood species, so neither is quoted here. One feature most reviews miss: a built-in power strip on the side panel, outlets, a red power switch, and a cord hook right next to it. Chargers and corded tools plug into the cabinet, not a strip draped across the floor. Outlet count is not published.

Setup

It moves on casters, visible in the listing photo, including a locking brake on at least one wheel — roll it to the project instead of carrying the project to the cabinet. A push handle on the same side lets you roll it across the shop. The honest catch: the listing publishes no caster size, count, or wheel weight rating, so there is no claim here about how it handles a cracked garage floor or how much loaded weight the wheels take. Same with the lock — there is a key lock on the front, above the top drawer, but the listing does not say whether one key locks all 15 drawers. If that matters, verify it before you buy.
