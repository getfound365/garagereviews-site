Indoor-safe — with rules, not wishes. The Little Buddy is CSA certified for indoor use, and the oxygen depletion sensor shuts the gas off if oxygen drops. That does not make it a set-and-forget heater: crack a window 1–2 inches for fresh air, keep 12 inches clear of walls and anything combustible, and never use it while sleeping — turn it off before bed in any tent, RV, or shelter. Carbon monoxide is odorless, and the sensor is a backup, not a substitute for ventilation.

Skip it if you need to heat a room: 3,800 BTU covers 95 sq ft and one person, not a garage. Skip it if you want adjustable heat or a bulk tank — the Portable Buddy MH9BX does both for $20 more. Performance in real use

The radiant ceramic burner does one job well: it throws infrared heat forward at the person facing it, so you feel warmth on your face and hands immediately and the air in a small shelter rises noticeably within 5–10 minutes. At 20°F outside, the product page says to expect 40–50°F inside a standard pop-up shelter — realistic for 3,800 BTU. The 5.6-hour runtime on a 1 lb cylinder is a full day on the ice or in the stand, and at about $0.50–$0.90 an hour in fuel it is cheaper than running an electric space heater on expensive power — and it works in an outage. It runs down to −40°F as long as the cylinder is not frozen; keep a spare inside your jacket in extreme cold. The 17 five-star reviews in the newest 25 repeat the same words — “great little heater,” “works better than I expected,” “super easy to setup” — and the 2-star side is almost entirely the igniter: hold the knob 20 seconds after the pilot lights, and if the flame goes out, wait 5 minutes before relighting.

Setup

Thread a 1 lb disposable propane cylinder clockwise onto the inlet valve until finger-tight — do not over-tighten, and listen for hissing that means a bad seal. Set it on a stable, level surface with 12 inches clear of walls and anything combustible, burner face away from flammable materials, and crack a window 1–2 inches before you light it. Turn the control knob to ON, press the red ignition button, and hold the knob for 20 seconds while the pilot establishes — the ceramic tile should glow orange-red within 30 seconds. If it goes out, wait 5 minutes and repeat. Above 7,000 feet it may take several lighting attempts. When you are done, turn the knob to OFF, let it cool 10 minutes before handling, and disconnect the cylinder for storage. And the rule that matters most: turn it off before you sleep, every time.
