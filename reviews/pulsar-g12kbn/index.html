The verdict

A lot of watts per dollar, with two honest asterisks. First, the newest 25 Walmart reviews are polarized — 17 five-star, but 6 one-star — and nearly all of the low reviews are about delivery, not the generator: freight damage, wrong-address delivery, and an 18-wheeler that couldn’t get down a driveway. The product itself draws praise for powering a large camper and a home. Second, this model has no CO shutoff — Pulsar’s similar PG12000BCO adds CO Sentry; the G12KBN does not list one. Best for: whole-home or large-RV backup where budget matters most and the owner will run it outdoors, far from the house, with CO detectors in place.

Who it's for

Homeowners who want to back up major appliances (well pump, furnace, fridge, freezer) on a budget, and large-RV owners who need 50-amp service and dual-fuel flexibility. Not for: anyone who wants a CO shutoff as a safety feature (buy the PG12000BCO instead), or anyone who can’t handle freight-style delivery of a 200+ lb unit.

Performance in real use

Buyers who got the unit running report strong performance. “This is a combination tool. It provides all the power our large camper needs, and it is also an emergency backup for our home. The dual-fuel capability was a major selling point for us,” wrote Kenneth (Aug 8, 2026). The low reviews in the newest 25 are dominated by logistics: a unit left unopened on a pallet with a carrier dispute (Russell, Oct 27, 2026), a package never received (jose, Oct 23, 2026), delivery by 18-wheeler that couldn’t reach the driveway (Deborah, Oct 16, 2026), and a forklift-damaged box arriving a month late (Francisco, Jun 19, 2026). Only one low review in the newest 25 is a price-credit complaint about the product itself, and that buyer added “The generator is a good product” (Michael, Apr 22, 2026). Read the rating honestly: the 4.3-star average is dragged down by freight and fulfillment, not by reported running failures.

Setup

Per the manual: add 10W-30 oil (37.2 oz), add gasoline or connect a propane tank with the included hose, and electric-start. The kit includes a propane hose, spark-plug wrench, 10mm wrench, and screwdriver, plus a mobility kit and battery. Expect a freight-style delivery — plan for a way to move a 200+ lb crate from the curb or driveway.

How it compares

Against the Westinghouse WGen3600c at $399, the G12KBN is a different job: whole-home power with dual fuel and a 50-amp outlet, at roughly double the price and weight class — and the WGen3600c has a CO sensor, which this one lacks. Against the WEN 56475 at $525, it is more than double the running watts (9,500 vs 3,750) with dual fuel and 240V output, at a higher price. See the full side-by-side on the comparison page.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts is the Pulsar G12KBN?

12,000 peak / 9,500 running watts on gasoline; 10,800 peak / 8,550 running watts on propane.

Does the G12KBN have a CO shutoff?

No. Neither Pulsar’s product page nor the manual lists a carbon monoxide shutoff for this model. Pulsar sells a similar 12,000-watt model, the PG12000BCO, with CO Sentry.

How long does it run on a tank?

Up to 12 hours at 50% load on gasoline from its 8-gallon tank, per the manual. Some Pulsar and retailer sources say 13 hours.

What outlets does it have?

Four 120V 20-amp outlets, one 120/240V 30-amp twist-lock, one 120/240V 50-amp outlet, and one 12V DC output.

What is the warranty?

Disputed: the manual and Amazon listing state a 1-year limited warranty, while Pulsar’s site states 3 years (with registration for extension). Confirm with Pulsar before relying on either figure.
