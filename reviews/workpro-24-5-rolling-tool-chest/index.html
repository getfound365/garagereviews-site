The verdict

Buy it for the size and the sale price, not for the build. Who it's for

Anyone setting up a first home garage who wants a full rolling chest at a sale price and can live with light-duty steel. Skip it if you need a pro-grade box that takes daily abuse — the 110 lb max load and the thin-metal reports say this is not that chest. Skip it if you cannot deal with a possible return: the damage-on-arrival rate in the newest reviews is high enough to plan around. And skip it if the 20-inch size fits your tools — that chest is $24 less for the same design.

Performance in real use

The drawers are the strong point: buyers repeat that they open and shut smoothly, and the chest earns 4.0 stars from 399 ratings on that basis. The two-piece design works as advertised — the top box detaches and slides into the bottom cabinet for storage, and the anti-theft bolt on the top box is a real feature for valuable tools. The weak points are the steel and the wheels: buyers call the metal thin, one reports a caster falling out and making the whole unit unstable, and the 110 lb max load means heavy items belong low or not at all. The open shelf in the bottom cabinet holds larger cases and buckets, which is where the storage actually lives.

Setup

It arrives partly assembled: attach the four casters, set the top box on the rolling cabinet, and check the lock. Do the inspection before you load it — open every drawer, test the keys, and look for dents in the lower door and the caster mounts, because those are the exact failure points buyers report. One buyer could not open the top box because the keys did not work; another found the bottom panel barely held on by three small screws. If anything is bent or missing, start the return right away rather than trying to bend it back.
