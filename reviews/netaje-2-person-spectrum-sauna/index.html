Who it’s for

Two people who want a home infrared sauna with app control and are happy to assemble a flat-pack cabin indoors. At 47.24 × 39.37 × 72.83 inches it needs a dedicated corner — a bedroom, home gym, or garage — and it is an indoor dry sauna, not a steam room. It is not for anyone who wants a verified maximum temperature or a deep review base before buying: neither exists on the listing.

Performance in real use

The listing’s claims are specific: five low-EMF carbon far-infrared panels reach 122°F in 10 minutes, with an upper heating panel wrapping the back and torso and a lower panel focused on the legs and feet, for uniform head-to-toe warmth. The cabin is red cedar with a 6-foot interior, a manual control panel plus Wi-Fi and Bluetooth app control for power, temperature, and music, a 7-color therapy light with nine options, two reading lights, and a Bluetooth speaker. Safety features are flame-retardant materials and a 30-minute auto shut-off. Buyer evidence is thin but consistent with the claims: Shonta (5 stars) reports a good sweat, and Maxwell (4 stars) reports sweating “pretty good” — but also two dead panels, which is the thing to check the day it arrives.

Setup

It ships as a knockdown cabin — panels that assemble in place — and the listing says all necessary parts are included. Plan the spot first: at 47.24 × 39.37 inches of floor and 72.83 inches tall, it does not go through a standard door assembled, so build it in the room where it will live. The $99.99 shipping fee applies at checkout. On arrival, power every panel and confirm all five heat before the return window closes — one buyer found two panels dead.
