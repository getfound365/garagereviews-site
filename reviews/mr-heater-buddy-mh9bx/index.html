Who it's for

Anyone who needs portable heat in a tent, garage, workshop, cabin, ice-fishing shanty, or as emergency backup heat — and who accepts the 1 lb cylinder routine or already owns a 20 lb tank and hose. Skip it if you want all-night heat without swapping cylinders, or if you won’t provide the fresh-air ventilation the manual requires indoors.

Performance in real use

Radiant heat, two settings, and no fan to fail or power: the Buddy throws heat at you and whatever is in front of it, which is why it works in a tent or a shanty where a fan heater would just stir cold air. The 4,000 BTU low setting stretches a 1 lb cylinder to 5.4 hours; high gives you 9,000 BTU for 2.4 hours. The piezo igniter lights it with a push, the swivel regulator lets the cylinder thread in without wrestling the heater, and the fold-down handle keeps it packable at 10.6 lb.

What buyers actually wrote lines up with the spec sheet: heat output as claimed, a well-made feel, and easy setup — one buyer had it hooked up in under 10 minutes and lit after about 15 seconds on pilot. The recurring complaints are the short cylinder life on high, the occasional dead-on-arrival unit (“It worked for about 1 day. Now it doesn’t work.” — Rexcraft68), and shipping delays. One review in the newest 25 is clearly misfiled — a 2-star complaint about broken eggs — which says more about Walmart’s review plumbing than about the heater.

Setup

Screw a 1 lb propane cylinder into the swivel regulator, turn the single control knob to pilot, and push the piezo igniter. Set it on a level surface with clear space around the hot front, and open fresh-air ventilation if you are using it indoors — the manual requires it, and a carbon monoxide detector belongs in the room. For a 20 lb tank, add the optional hose and filter; a 5-ft hose and adapter come with the separate $99.59 bundle variant if you buy that one instead.
