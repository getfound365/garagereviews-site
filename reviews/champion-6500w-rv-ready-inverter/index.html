The verdict

This is the strongest-reviewed generator in today’s set and the lead pick for a reason: 4.9 stars from 115 ratings on Walmart, $999, in stock, and a spec sheet that answers the exact question the storm is asking — can it keep the essentials alive? At 5,000 running watts it covers a fridge, a freezer, a furnace fan, lights, a TV, and a coffee maker at once, and buyers who actually ran it through outages say exactly that. One buyer ran a fridge, freezer, furnace, TV, coffee maker, computer, and “many of my lights” off it and called it “very quiet compared to my old one.”

The honest catch is fuel: it is gasoline only. When the power is out, gas stations run on power too, and a dual-fuel unit that also burns propane has a second way to keep going. If you already keep a gas can rotated and full, that is a manageable trade for $999 and the strongest review base in the class. If you want propane backup, the Pulsar PGX60BiSRCO at $799 is the cheaper dual-fuel alternative — but it gives up 500 running watts to get there.

Who it’s for

Homeowners and RVers who need the outage essentials covered and want the quietest big inverter in the $999 band. The TT-30R RV outlet and the 120/240V locking outlet mean it plugs into a camper or a transfer switch without adapters, and 64 dB(A) at 23 feet is quiet enough that neighbors won’t hate you at 2 a.m. It is not for anyone who needs propane — there is no propane option on this unit — and it is not a whole-home unit: 5,000 running watts will not carry a central air conditioner or an electric water heater.

Performance in real use

The buyer evidence is the best in the set, and it is consistent: quiet, easy to move, and enough power for the whole essentials list. Mares (5 stars, Nov 4, 2026) bought it for a 20-foot RV: “very quiet and powerful and easy to carry and very stable.” Odupre (5 stars, Apr 30, 2026) ran a fridge, freezer, furnace, TV, coffee maker, computer, and lights, and the only drawback was that “it seems to take about 5 pulls to get it started after sitting for more than week.” Tippertom (5 stars, Apr 7, 2026) bought it for a trailer and was “impressed with how long the fuel lasts.” The one caution in the newest reviews is from kh2026 (4 stars, Mar 28, 2026): “a little louder than I expected” — worth hearing, because 64 dB(A) is a manufacturer figure measured at 23 feet, and in a small yard it will still sound like a generator.

On paper, the 18-hour runtime at 25% load is the number that matters in a multi-day outage: at quarter load — fridge cycling, lights, fan — a full tank carries you overnight and into the next day. The clean power (under 3% THD) is safe for laptops, phones, and medical devices, per Champion.

Setup

Add oil, add gas, and use the EZ Start Dial — Economy Mode stretches the tank when the load is light. The RV-ready TT-30R outlet plugs straight into a camper; the 120/240V locking outlet is the one for a transfer switch or a heavy extension cord. CO Shield watches carbon monoxide and shuts the engine down if it climbs — but that is a backup, not a plan: run it outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house, with the exhaust pointed away from windows and doors, and never in a garage, even with the door open. Keep a battery CO alarm in the house whenever a generator is running.
