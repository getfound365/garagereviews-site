The verdict

Buy it at $99.99. This is the cheapest route to solar cellular scouting with a genuinely free tier: the SPLB-10 panel and its 15,000 mAh battery end the battery-swap routine, the microSD card is already in the box, and the free plan sends 100 photos a month with no fees and no commitment. Buyers give it 4.4 stars from 49 ratings, and the Bazaarvoice cross-check is 4.52 from 27 reviews — a subset of the Walmart count, so this page shows the Walmart figure and the live stats call stays off.

The honest catch is what $99.99 doesn’t buy: 720p video, not 1080p, and 28MP stills that are interpolated from a smaller sensor — independent testing scored the image quality 79/100 against a 93/100 detection circuit. The free plan is real but thin: one buyer, EVERETT, reports, “This camera freakin rocks. The only down side is the free subscription. 100 photos you burn through 25 of them just getting it dialed into your hunting zone.” Heavy use means a paid plan, and the review base is thin at 49 ratings.

Who it's for

Anyone running cameras in remote spots who hates battery swaps, and anyone building a multi-camera setup where a free 100-photo tier per camera keeps the running cost at zero. Skip it if you need 1080p video or 4K photos (that’s the Tactacam X 3.0), if you scout a high-traffic spot that will blow through 100 photos in days, or if any subscription at all is a deal-breaker.

Performance in real use

The camera itself is the budget pick in SPYPOINT’s line for a reason: 28MP photos, 720p video with sound, a 0.4-second trigger (Walmart’s listing says 0.45s), a 90 ft detection and flash range on four low-glow LEDs, and a 3-image burst mode. Constant Capture sends and captures at the same time, so the camera isn’t blind while it uploads. True Dual SIM technology auto-connects to the strongest of the major networks, GPS tags the camera’s location, and four capture modes — Photo, Video, Time-Lapse, and Time-Lapse+ — cover scouting and property monitoring alike.

The bundle is the story, though: the SPLB-10 compact solar panel carries a 15,000 mAh battery that SPYPOINT says fully charges in about 24 hours of sunlight or 5 hours with a power adapter, and buyers report the set-and-forget part actually working — “Takes great pictures, and with the solar charger and cell coverage it is a set and for get it.” The complaints to weigh are the thin free plan, a paid subscription that one buyer says kept dropping, and the occasional low-signal failure to send. The camera is IP65 water-resistant and takes 8 AA batteries or a LIT-22 lithium pack as backup power.

Setup

Mount it with the included strap and arm, facing north if you can — sun glare is the enemy of every trail camera. Activate it in the SPYPOINT app, format the included microSD card with the on-camera buttons, and take a test photo. Set the sensitivity low at first: every false trigger during setup eats into the free 100 photos. Then check that the first photos land on your phone before you walk away.
