Performance in real use

Three settings instead of two is the whole point: 4,000 BTU sips a cylinder for 10.8 hours, 9,000 BTU runs 4.8 hours, and 18,000 BTU runs 2.4 hours, all from two 1 lb cylinders. A 20 lb tank stretches that to days — listings quote 96 to 108 hours on low, depending on the source. The built-in fan is the other upgrade over the little Buddy: it circulates the radiant heat instead of letting it pool at the ceiling, and it runs off an A/C adapter or 4 D-cell batteries.

What buyers actually wrote is consistent: lots of heat, a well-made heater, a good price, and the fan doing its job. The 1-star tail in the newest reviews is almost entirely shipping — “I never received it” and delayed deliveries — not the heater itself. One buyer bought it as apartment backup heat for a long power cut, which is exactly the emergency-indoor use the manual allows on 1 lb cylinders.

Setup

Screw in one or two 1 lb propane cylinders — or connect a 20 lb tank with an optional hose and filter. Power the fan with the A/C adapter or 4 D-cell batteries. Set it on a level surface with clear space around the hot front, and give it the ventilation the manual requires indoors: at least 18 sq in of vent area, for example an opening about 4¼ by 4¼ inches, plus a working carbon monoxide detector. Emergency indoor heating is for disposable 1 lb cylinders only, per the manual — not a plumbed 20 lb setup.
