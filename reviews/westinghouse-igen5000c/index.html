The verdict

Buy it if you want a 5,000-watt gas inverter with a CO sensor and remote start for an RV or a home outage, and you do not need propane. At $899 it is $50 more than the gas-only iGen5000 without the sensor — the CO sensor is the difference — and $100 less than the dual-fuel Champion 201319. Buyers give it 4.7 stars from 7,533 ratings, one of the largest review bases in this batch, and the words that keep showing up are “ready to go 5 minutes out of the box,” “very quiet,” and “love the remote start.” One buyer calls it “top of its class” after comparing everything in its class.

The honest catches: it is gas-only, so a propane tank will not help you in an outage, and the review base includes one buyer whose unit dropped an exhaust valve after 4–5 months. At 105.8 pounds it is not a one-person carry. If you want propane, the Champion 201319 at $999 is the move; if you want the sensor for $50 less, there is no cheaper CO-sensor option — the iGen5000 at $849 has no sensor at all.

Who it's for

Anyone with an RV or a home outage kit who wants 5,000 peak watts, a CO sensor, and remote start, and who runs on gasoline. Skip it if you need propane: this model is gas-only. Skip it if you want the sensor for less: the $849 iGen5000 has no CO sensor, and there is no cheaper CO-sensor inverter in this batch.

Performance in real use

The 224cc engine makes 5,000 peak / 3,900 running watts, with a 3.4-gallon tank and up to 18 hours at 25% load — about 0.19 gallons per hour. At 52 dBA it is quiet for its class, and under 3% THD means sensitive electronics are safe on it. The control panel carries 3 AC outlets including the TT-30R RV outlet, plus a digital display. Remote start comes with a key fob — buyers call it out by name: “Love the remote start!” One buyer reports it “ready to go 5 minutes out of the box” with the battery, oil, and funnel included. The one serious complaint in the newest reviews is a dropped exhaust valve after months of use — a single report, but the kind worth knowing about.

Setup

Recoil, electric, and remote start — the key fob is included. Fill with the enclosed oil using the enclosed funnel, fill with gas, and go: one buyer did it in five minutes. At 105.8 pounds, plan a ramp or a second person to load it. Break it in before the outage and test the remote start from inside the RV — the fob is the feature buyers praise most, and the first outage is the wrong time to learn its range.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts does the Westinghouse iGen5000c make?

5,000 peak / 3,900 running watts on gasoline.

Is the iGen5000c dual fuel?

No — it is gas-only. The dual-fuel iGen5000DFc is a different model, and the gas-only iGen5000 without a CO sensor is $849.

Does it have a CO sensor?

Yes — the “c” is the CO sensor. It shuts the engine down if carbon monoxide builds up. It is not a substitute for a CO alarm or for running the generator outdoors.

Does it have remote start?

Yes — recoil, electric, and remote start, with a key fob included. Buyers call the remote start out by name.
